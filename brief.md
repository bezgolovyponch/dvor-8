# brief.md — DVOR-8 intake

**Project: DVOR-8** (двор — the yard seen from a balcony).
Run A of the Game Skeleton Master Prompt. Every answer is tagged per rule 3. The creator's words are quoted;
nothing is paraphrased into something tidier.

## Round 1 — definitions

### Q1. Spectral collision — **CREATOR, confirmed as written**
The creator accepted the working definition unchanged:

> A hook is described by a set of spectra. Each spectrum is one continuous design axis, for example outcome
> certainty from fully predictable to fully random. A collision is the moment in play when the game's
> positions on two or more spectra meet at once and produce the engagement effect. The formula names the
> spectra, their positions, the collision event, the psychological mechanism and the player's response.

Nothing was replaced, so section 6 terms stand and **Run D is unblocked.**

### Q2. Legendary — **CREATOR, accepted as offered**
The three-part test in Phase 3 is accepted: **documented origin, documented adoption by later games,
documented effect on player behaviour.** All three must be sourced, or the case is downgraded. The hook list
is still shown at gate G3 for adding or striking cases.

### Q3. 8-bit — **CREATOR: visual style only**
Not hard limits. **No** fixed base resolution, **no** counted palette, **no** sprite ceiling, **no**
two-button input, **no** chiptune obligation. "8-bit" describes how DVOR-8 looks, not what it runs on.

### Q4. Scenario strategies — **CREATOR: the scan converts reality into a game you then play**
The scan is the opening. The 8-bit world becomes a playable level with its own rules. Narrative structure,
encounter scripting and long-term pacing all follow from that. **DVOR-8 therefore has a story — the yard's
— and free-form conversation is not its form.**

## Round 2 — product

### Q5. Platform and session — **CREATOR: web first, mobile later**
Browser first, the way the previous project runs: no install, a local server, no build step. Mobile is a
later target, so no mechanic may depend on platform APIs that only exist on a phone.

### Q6. Audience — **CREATOR: 18+, worldwide**
Adults, worldwide. Store and advertising rules for every region therefore apply, and the drunkenness and
destruction in the concept are inside the allowed range but subject to those rules.

### Q7. Business model — **CREATOR: none yet**
No hook may exist to serve a business model. This agrees with the ethics answer below and makes the
rejection of paid randomness structural rather than a preference.

### Q8. Ethical line — **CREATOR: waiting is allowed; loss and paid randomness are not**
Hooks may rely on **real-time waiting** as a mechanic. They may **not** rely on punishing loss. They may
**not** rely on paid randomness. Under rule 10, a hook found in Phase 3 that depends on loss threat or paid
randomness is **rejected with that reason recorded**, not adapted.

## Round 3 — scope

### Q9. Genre — **CREATOR: sandbox destruction**
Destroying things is central, and it carries consequences. Not an observation sim, not an unlock explorer:
those may be present, but the genre is sandbox destruction.

### Q10. Production — **CREATOR: the agent and the creator, vanilla web, no engine**
Modules and plain files, as the previous project is built. Used only to filter out mechanics that cannot be
built this way; a mechanic requiring a 3D engine or a team of artists is out of scope at intake.

### Q11. Storage — **CREATOR: Markdown and JSON in `/Users/m/DVOR-8/game-skeleton/`**
The seven files: `brief.md`, `sources.json`, `atoms.json`, `hooks.json`, `formula.md`, `questions.md`,
`skeleton.md`.
