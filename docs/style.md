# Style — findings, decisions and tests

The look and the feel of DVOR-8, what each part was taken from, and the test that holds it.

Tags: `SOURCED[n]` — stated on a page that was opened (list at the end). `SNIPPET` — seen only in a search
result, page not opened. `DERIVED[Fn]` — inferred from findings. `CHOICE` — this project's decision, no
external source; numbers set by eye.

| F17 | **The trauma model of camera shake.** Eiserloh, "Juicing Your Cameras With Math", GDC 2016, verbatim: "Maintain a 'trauma' level in [0,1] · Damage, stress adds trauma (+= 0.2 or 0.5) · Trauma decreases (linearly) over time · Camera shake is trauma² or trauma³". | F24 | **Ordered dithering was the wrong choice for this palette.** Surma's "Ditherpunk", verbatim: "error diffusion algorithms ... can handle arbitrary color palettes, while ordered dithering requires your color palette to be evenly spaced." F-S matrix as printed: `1/16 · ( * 7 / 3 5 1 )`. | `VERIFIED` — opened |
| F25 | **Edge-preserving smoothing, and which one.** OpenCV on the bilateral filter, verbatim: sigma values "< 10" have little effect and "> 150" make the image "cartoonish"; "Large filters (d > 5) are very slow, so it is recommended to use d=5 for real-time". Parameters in the paper's Fig.1: r = 2/4/8, eps = 0.12/0.22/0.42 on input in [0,1]. | `VERIFIED` — OpenCV docs + the ECCV PDF |

## Decisions

Rewritten 2026-10-11 for the third build. What changed from the second, and why, is in `docs/concept.md`.

| # | Decision | Tag | Held by test |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The yard is stored as palette indices and looked up through a table each frame. A filter's look is another table. | DERIVED[F1, F3] | *sober shows the yard's own table* |
| D2 | **The table is made from the photograph**: its 28 best colours by median cut, after a grade — contrast stretched to the photo's own range, saturation × 1.45, shadows pulled toward purple, lights toward warm white. The second build mapped brightness onto two fixed neon ramps, which turned every yard into the same heat-map. | CHOICE; "high-contrast" from F8, F9 | *a red car is red* · *a blue car is blue* · *a green tree is green* · *deep shadows and bright lights, shadows toward purple* |
| D3 | The picture is 192 × 256 pixels, about an 8-bit console's screen, with ordered dithering so plain surfaces keep texture. | DERIVED[F9]; size CHOICE | *the size of an 8-bit console screen* · *most of the palette is in use* |
| D4 | **Targets are real things found by an object detector on the device** (EfficientDet-Lite0 through MediaPipe, vendored). Nothing is uploaded. What the detector cannot name is not a target; the game invents nothing to hit. | SOURCED[F16]; the choice of model is CHOICE | `tools/check.mjs` on real photographs · *every found thing becomes a target with a name* |
| D5 | The photograph is searched whole and in 13 overlapping slices, because a car from a balcony is too small to survive shrinking the whole photo to the model's input. | SOURCED[F14] | *searched whole and in overlapping slices* · *one car seen in two slices is one car* |
| D6 | People and animals are never targets and never named. | CHOICE, from the brief's "not violent" | *people and animals are never targets* |
| D7 | A target's pixels are those in its box whose colour is unlike the ring just outside the box; if that fails, a rounded box. Each target gets a dark outline. | CHOICE | *a target's pixels are the thing, not the asphalt* · *a dark line round it, like a sprite* |
| D8 | A wrecked thing stays where it was, dark and broken, and its pieces come to rest at its own foot. | SOURCED[F9]; F13 SNIPPET | *what is left is dark, but still where the car was* · *what falls STAYS, at the foot of the thing* |
| D9 | The frame tilts about 1° all the time; scanlines; hard pink and cyan offset shadows; nothing rounded. | F10 SNIPPET, F11; details CHOICE | *the frame always leans a little* |
| D10 | **Rewritten 2026-10-12 on a sourced module.** A tap is answered in the same frame. Hit pause **50 ms**, wreck pause **110 ms** — moved toward Tomizawa & Ishikawa's measured pleasant band (0.37 s pleasant, 0.537 s boundary) but deliberately NOT to it, because Kao's inverted-U still applies. Shake is **Eiserloh's trauma model**: trauma accumulates per hit (0.3 / 0.45 / 0.6), decays **linearly** at 0.5/s, and the offset is `trauma³ × 7 px`, capped at 7. Camera rotation rides the same trauma. | `SOURCED[F17, F18]`; the amplitudes are CHOICE scaled from Bevy | *a tap is answered in the same frame* · *a dent, a crack and a break are felt differently* · *shake is the cube of trauma* · *within the stated maximum* |
| D11 | The amounts in D10 are kept moderate on purpose and capped, and the trauma decay is **linear** so a jolt has a defined end rather than a tail. | F15, F17 | *many wrecks at once do not shake the picture off the screen* · *a fully decayed shake is exactly zero* |
| D12 | **A dent, a crack and a break are felt differently on every channel** — freeze, flash, trauma and ring. Before this they were identical, which in a game about hitting things is the most expensive kind of sameness: the player cannot feel progress toward a break. | DERIVED; F18 supports hit-pause generally | *a dent, a crack and a break are felt differently on every channel* |
| D12 | Hits in quick succession build a combo that multiplies points; it runs out on its own; a miss costs nothing. A best score is kept on the device. | SOURCED[F8]; no penalty from brief Q8 | *hits in quick succession build a combo* · *nothing in any mode takes anything away* |
| D13 | «ЕЩЁ РАЗ» restarts the same yard at once, without searching the photograph again. | SOURCED[F8, F9] | *«ещё раз» is a clean yard* |
| D14 | **Every filter is a way to play, not only a look.** Each changes what is in the hand and what can be seen clearly. This reverses the second build's "a filter is a look, never a rule", which was reported as "the filters don't change anything". | CHOICE, at the creator's direction | *every mode but sober changes BOTH the throw and the focus* · *no two modes put the same thing in the hand* |
| D15 | **Sober — a stone.** Lands where aimed, 110 ms, everything sharp. | CHOICE | *sober: the throw lands exactly where it was aimed* |
| D16 | **Weed — a slipper.** Flies slowly, then skips to up to two more things; a combo lasts 5 s; the edges of the yard go soft; colours richer and drifting. | look DERIVED[F4]; play CHOICE | *the slipper skips on* · *a combo forgives a pause twice as long* · *the colours keep drifting* |
| D17 | **Alcohol — a bottle.** Bursts over everything within 22 px, but lands up to 10 px from where it was aimed; nothing is in focus; warm, leaning, double vision. | look SOURCED[F6, F7]; play CHOICE | *a bottle bursts over everything near* · *the hand strays, never further than the stated scatter* |

| D48 | **The whole environment is destructible, in the same language as the things.** A hit dents: the pixels near the impact move one tone down their OWN ramp, never to black. Enough hits tear a ragged hole, written into the rubble layer so it is permanent (D8). Toughness is keyed to the ramp the painter already chose — leaves 1, soil and painted walls 2, concrete and asphalt 3 — so nothing new is classified. | CHOICE, from D7 and D8; the minimal wreck answers a measured failure, since a whole-thing scorch stopped resembling the photograph | *enough hits wreck it: what is left is darker, still where the car was, and torn open* · a broken surface cannot be hit again |
| D49 | **Scenery gives points but not combo.** The yard is worth breaking for the hole, not for the multiplier: with scenery feeding the combo, the cheapest route to a big score would be to hammer the ground and ignore the things the detector found. | CHOICE | the environment hit path adds score and never touches `world.combo` |
| D50 | **The day's sky.** Rain, snow, fog or a storm is rolled from the date like the street (D28), and it is a GRADE on the palette table before it is any particle — a whole-screen mood is a different table (D1). People hurry in rain and dawdle in snow; lightning blackens one standing thing and has no code path to a person. | CHOICE for the numbers; the directions (a wet surface is darker, snow is a diffuse light, fog eats contrast with distance) are sourced | *the weather is a GRADE on the table: rain darkens and cools, snow lifts and flattens* · *lightning can only choose a THING* |

| D51 | **The r9 vector route: flat shapes with straight edges, one colour per shape** (`src/vector.js`), behind `?style=vector`. Per-pixel painting was faithful and unstable — 13–15% stray pixels, 18–19% specks under 4 px, pixel-jagged borders, and facades merged across weak seams. This route traces each shape's outline, simplifies it into a polyline, fills it back, and gives the shape ONE colour. It also brings the sky in: a blue core grown through its own clouds, stopped at the skyline, plus designed clouds and a single skyline line. | CHOICE informed by measurement; the four failure modes are MEASURED on the three photographs | *stray pixels 13–15% → 0.04–0.09%* · *specks under four pixels 18–19% → 0.22–0.36%* · *blobs per 1000 px 19–23 → 2.0–4.2* · 272 checks, 0 FAIL |
| D52 | **Windows are an open item, and are written down as one.** The local-contrast window finder returns 1–2 windows per photograph where a street facade holds dozens. The geometry route in `docs/style-candidates.md` (rectify, then fit the grid) is the researched answer and is not built; the OpenCV.js experiment that would do it lives in the lab (`facade-geometry.js`) because it needs a 10.9 MB download the game does not ship. | MEASURED failure, honestly recorded rather than papered over | *windows 1 / 2 / 1 on the three photographs* |
| D53 | **Enhance first, then recognise: shadows are demoted from boundary to tone.** `src/enhance.js` divides out a large-radius blur (the lighting), opens the local contrast, and smooths the grain; the vector route takes its SHAPE labels from the flattened luma, so the lit and shadowed halves of one wall become one shape, while the colours painted still come from the original pixels. The straight-line density the same module measures is what separates sky from a distant city — the scene analysis found depth alone cannot, and it is right. | MEASURED: shape count and stability; the principle is from the scene analysis (docs/scene-analysis.md) | *shapes straightened 228-284 → 259-399* · stray pixels stay at 0.09-0.10% |
| D54 | **The detector is EfficientDet-Lite2, not Lite0.** Measured in the scene analysis on this game's own photographs: 13 cars where Lite0 found 8 on photo 2, no facade called a truck on photo 3, and no concrete wall called a boat on photo 1, for the same 7.5 MB on disk and about 1.5 s more per photograph. | MEASURED (scene analysis); the model is vendored and the swap is one argument | *efficientdet_lite2.tflite, 7,515,971 bytes, int8* |
| D55 | **A facade is a RHYTHM, and its unit is the structure, not the colour.** Windows went from 1/2/1 per photograph to **22/15/15** when the wall was taken from the structure (flat, bright, not sky, not foliage) and the openings were read as periodic peaks in the wall's own row and column profiles, instead of thresholding colour shapes — which fragmented a wall into pieces too small to hold a facade. | MEASURED, three photographs | *windows 1/2/1 → 22/15/15* · the grid is drawn with a one-pixel frame so a window reads as a window |
